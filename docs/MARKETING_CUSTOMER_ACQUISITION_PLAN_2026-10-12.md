**Status:** Planning only — no posts, ads, website changes or spending authorized  
**Prepared:** 2026-10-12  
**Market:** Bolivar, Missouri and nearby communities Brittany chooses to serve

## 1. Situation and objective

Brittany reports two challenges: (1) not enough people contact Aunt B's; (2) some who do contact her do not follow through. **The primary bottleneck is inquiry volume.** Do not reflexively lower prices to solve a discoverability problem.

The business is bootstrapped, with no confirmed state/federal registration, formal insurance, or advertising budget as of this discussion. Plan for **$0 paid advertising** initially. Do not advertise as licensed, bonded, insured, or background checked without verification. Childcare, pet care, house sitting and cleaning have different safety, licensing, insurance and customer-trust considerations; confirm requirements and coverage before expanding services or hiring.

**30-day aim:** Make the offer understandable, increase qualified local inquiries, respond consistently, and learn which free channels produce bookings. No guaranteed lead count or revenue promise.

## 4. Free/low-cost acquisition channels

### A. Facebook page + local groups
- Post from the business page regularly; share into relevant Bolivar/nearby groups **only where group rules permit promotions**.
- Avoid identical copy pasted across many groups in a short burst.
- Use a distinct post for each service, with a helpful scenario and direct CTA.
- Answer legitimate questions promptly; never pressure families or promise unavailable dates.
- Personal referrals from friends/family can help, but prioritize people who actually live in the service area.

### B. Referrals and repeat clients
- After successful work, ask whether the customer would be comfortable recommending Brittany or leaving an honest review.
- Ask cleaning clients about upcoming pet care or house sitting needs, and vice versa, without aggressive upselling.
- Offer a simple follow-up check-in for recurring cleaning prospects.
- Keep a list of customers who have consented to future contact; honor opt-outs.

### C. Google/local search
- Investigate eligibility for a **free Google Business Profile** as a service-area business, using truthful business details and applicable verification rules.
- Hide residential address when appropriate; do not create fake locations or multiple duplicate listings.
- Confirm service categories and whether mixed childcare/home services fit listing policies before publishing.
- Seek honest customer reviews and maintain consistent business name/contact/service area across listings.

### D. Community and partnerships
- Ask permission to place a simple flyer or card on local community boards.
- Explore appropriate relationships with real estate agents, landlords, pet owners and local family networks.
- Avoid claiming endorsements, partnerships or affiliations that have not been agreed.
- QR code should lead to a tested mobile-friendly landing page.

### E. Care.com / marketplaces
- Keep the service profile accurate and complete; check platform terms and fees.
- Track inquiries and conversions by platform, but avoid over-reliance on one marketplace.
- Follow platform rules for contact, safety and payments.

## 6. Suggested posting cadence

Keep it sustainable: approximately **3 helpful posts per week**, not an exhausting daily schedule.

| Post type | Example angle | Call to action |
|---|---|---|
| Service spotlight | What a standard cleaning visit includes | Message for a personalized quote |
| Owner/trust | Meet Aunt B; her experience and service philosophy | Ask about availability |
| Situation-based | Need someone to check on pets/plants while away? | Send dates and neighborhood |
| Customer proof (with permission) | Before/after cleaning photo or review | Request a quote |
| Availability update | Limited openings only if actually available | Message to check dates |

Rotate content and avoid publicly displaying prices as universal guarantees when scope varies.

## 7. Inquiry handling and follow-through

**Response process:**
1. Acknowledge inquiry promptly when possible (do not promise instant response).
2. Confirm service, general area, dates/times, number of people/pets where relevant, and broad scope.
3. Clarify needs with a short service-specific intake; keep sensitive details in restricted-access forms.
4. Provide a clear written quote or rate basis and what is/isn't included.
5. If no response, send one courteous follow-up after a reasonable interval; don't spam.
6. On agreement, document scope, schedule, cancellation terms and payment method.

**Example initial response:**
> Hi! Thanks for reaching out to Aunt B's. I'd be happy to learn more. What service are you looking for, what area are you in, and what dates or times do you need help? Once I have a few details, I can check availability and discuss pricing with you.

**Example gentle follow-up:**
> Hi! Just checking whether you're still looking for help with [service]. No pressure at all — feel free to message me if you have questions or would like to discuss dates.

GrowthAI may eventually help draft responses and negotiation messages, but Brittany reviews and approves them. Avoid automatic commitments on price, availability, or childcare safety.

## 8. Simple measurement system

Track **inquiries**, not likes or impressions alone. Use a private sheet or future ServicesOS lead records with:

| Field | Example / purpose |
|---|---|
| Inquiry date | When message arrived |
| Service requested | Cleaning / childcare / pet care / house sitting |
| Source (technical) | Website / Facebook / marketplace / direct |
| Source (customer-reported) | Facebook group, referral, Google, etc. |
| City / general area | Travel feasibility; no public street address |
| Status | New → Contacted → Qualified → Quoted → Booked / Lost |
| Quoted amount | If provided |
| Booking value | If booked |
| Lost reason | No response / price / availability / out of area / other |
| Follow-up date | Next appropriate contact |

**Weekly metrics:**
- New inquiries by service and channel.
- Response time where measurable.
- Quotes issued.
- Bookings and booked revenue.
- Inquiry-to-quote and quote-to-booking conversion rates (only when denominators are nonzero).
- Reasons prospects didn't proceed.

Do not place detailed children's health information, access codes, or private family information in the marketing tracker.

## 9. Free marketing vs. spending decision

Start with no paid advertising. Consider a small test only after:
- The website and contact funnel have been tested end-to-end.
- Brittany can respond to additional leads.
- Service boundaries, business requirements and appropriate insurance are addressed.
- At least a few weeks of baseline inquiry and conversion data exist.
- There is a clear budget and stop-loss threshold approved by Brittany.

Do not infer that a boosted post will solve low conversions.

## 10. Risks and open decisions

- **Business setup:** confirm registration, taxes, local requirements, childcare obligations, and appropriate insurance before advertising expanded or staffed services.
- **Trust claims:** don't imply licensing, insurance, background checks or professional certifications that have not been verified.
- **Customer privacy:** never use client/child images, reviews or identifiable job details without appropriate consent.
- **Social accounts:** verify whether Facebook's displayed personal-profile fields reflect the business page correctly; remove irrelevant personal fields if they are public and distracting.
- **Brand naming:** Aunt B's is Brittany's long-standing nickname; consumer-facing descriptor can evolve without implying a legal name change.
- **Site URL:** select one verified canonical link; fix inconsistent URLs on Facebook.
- **Availability:** advertise only services Brittany can currently deliver.
- **Budget:** no spending or live ad launch is approved by this plan.
